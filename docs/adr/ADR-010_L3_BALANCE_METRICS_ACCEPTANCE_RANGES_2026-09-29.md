# ADR-010 — L3 Balance Metrics and Acceptance Ranges — 2026-09-29

## Status

**Accepted as initial L3 simulation acceptance baseline / tunable balance criteria**

This ADR defines how the initial Operational Cost and Gross Reward models from ADR-008 and ADR-009 are evaluated.

It does not select M6 scope, create CP-50+, or freeze final production balance.

## Goal

Translate the L0 principle **Solo Competitiveness = Low Operational Cost** into measurable L3 acceptance criteria without requiring Solo to have the highest gross reward.

The initial simulator must be able to distinguish:

- formation total output,
- individual efficiency,
- time efficiency,
- Gold operating burden,
- duration-dependent comparative advantage.

The objective is not to force one universally optimal formation. The objective is to preserve meaningful trade-offs.

## Reference duration values

For the current initial model only:

| Duration | Elapsed time |
|---|---:|
| Short | 0.5 h |
| Medium | 2 h |
| Long | 8 h |

These remain tunable and are not production commitments.

## Required simulator metrics

### 1. Gross formation reward

```text
grossFormationReward
```

Total Gold-equivalent output generated by the formation before Operational Cost.

### 2. Effective operational Gold cost

```text
effectiveOperationalGoldCost
```

Gold-equivalent Operational Cost after applicable reductions, excluding unrelated route/content fees unless the scenario explicitly includes them.

### 3. Net formation reward

```text
netFormationReward =
  grossFormationReward - effectiveOperationalGoldCost
```

### 4. Net reward per participant

```text
netRewardPerParticipant =
  netFormationReward / participantCount
```

This is a primary Solo-competitiveness metric.

### 5. Net reward per hour

For total formation output:

```text
netRewardPerHour =
  netFormationReward / durationHours
```

### 6. Net reward per participant per hour

```text
netRewardPerParticipantPerHour =
  netFormationReward / participantCount / durationHours
```

This is the primary comparison metric for efficient individual participation.

### 7. Operational cost share

```text
operationalCostShare =
  effectiveOperationalGoldCost / grossFormationReward
```

This is diagnostic rather than a hard product invariant. It shows whether a formation's cost burden is materially affecting its economics.

### 8. Relative gross-output advantage

Against Solo at the same duration:

```text
relativeGrossAdvantage =
  groupGrossReward / soloGrossReward
```

This is used to verify that larger formations' gross-output advantage generally decreases with longer duration.

### 9. Relative individual-efficiency advantage

Against Solo at the same duration:

```text
relativePerParticipantEfficiency =
  groupNetRewardPerParticipant / soloNetRewardPerParticipant
```

and similarly for per-hour values.

### 10. Sustainable repetition under a Gold budget

For finite budget `B`:

```text
repeatCount = floor(B / effectiveOperationalGoldCost)
```

For zero-cost Solo formation overhead, this metric is treated as unbounded with respect to formation Operational Cost alone and must not be used as a standalone ranking metric.

Travel/content/stamina systems may later create additional limiting resources.

## Metrics intentionally not used as standalone acceptance gates

### Reward / Operational Cost

Because Solo formation Operational Cost is currently zero, the direct ratio is undefined/infinite for Solo.

Therefore:

```text
reward / operationalCost
```

must not be the sole efficiency metric.

It may become useful in scenarios where all formations pay a non-zero total Gold requirement after Travel/content costs.

## Initial acceptance ranges

The following ranges define the first balance-evaluation baseline.

They are intentionally broad enough to allow different strategic roles while protecting ADR-006.

### A. Total-output incentive for grouping

At each comparable duration:

```text
Party net total > Solo net total
Caravan net total > Party net total
```

Purpose:

- preserve a reason to assemble larger formations,
- avoid making Solo universally optimal on total output.

### B. Short-duration Solo competitiveness

For Short operations, Solo may lose narrowly on per-participant efficiency, but must remain close to the best group case.

Required:

```text
Solo net reward per participant
  >= 90% of best group net reward per participant
```

and:

```text
Solo net reward per participant per hour
  >= 90% of best group value
```

This permits short coordinated operations to have a modest efficiency premium without invalidating Solo play.

### C. Medium-duration Solo efficiency

For Medium operations:

```text
Solo net reward per participant
  >= best group net reward per participant
```

and:

```text
Solo net reward per participant per hour
  >= best group value
```

This is the first point where Solo should normally become individually more efficient.

### D. Long-duration Solo efficiency

For Long operations:

```text
Solo net reward per participant
  > best group net reward per participant
```

and:

```text
Solo net reward per participant per hour
  > best group value
```

Long-duration operation is a core Solo specialization under ADR-006.

### E. Larger-formation gross advantage must shrink with duration

For each larger formation class:

```text
relativeGrossAdvantage(Short)
  >= relativeGrossAdvantage(Medium)
  >= relativeGrossAdvantage(Long)
```

Strict inequality is preferred for the initial baseline but not required for every future specialized content scenario.

### F. Operational Cost must remain economically visible

Party and Caravan Operational Cost must be greater than zero before reduction under ordinary scenarios.

For the initial baseline:

```text
Party cost > 0
Caravan cost > Party cost
```

A specialized build may reduce effective cost to zero, but the unreduced base requirement must remain observable.

### G. No universal group dominance

A candidate balance fails if Party or Caravan simultaneously dominates Solo across all of the following for Short, Medium, and Long:

- net reward per participant,
- net reward per participant per hour,
- lower/equal Operational Cost burden per participant.

This is the direct L3 enforcement of ADR-006.

### H. No universal Solo dominance

A candidate balance also fails if Solo dominates Party/Caravan in both:

- total net formation reward,
- per-participant efficiency,

across substantially all comparable scenarios.

The purpose is strategic differentiation, not Solo supremacy.

## Current baseline check

Using ADR-008 / ADR-009 with no reductions, Travel Cost, or content cost:

### Net total

| Formation | Short | Medium | Long |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Solo | 10 | 40 | 120 |
| Party | 21 | 67 | 172 |
| Caravan | 41 | 108 | 228 |

Total-output ordering passes:

```text
Solo < Party < Caravan
```

for all three durations.

### Solo net reward per participant

| Duration | Solo |
|---|---:|
| Short | 10 |
| Medium | 40 |
| Long | 120 |

### Best Party case (2 participants)

| Duration | Party total | Per participant |
|---|---:|---:|
| Short | 21 | 10.5 |
| Medium | 67 | 33.5 |
| Long | 172 | 86.0 |

### Best Caravan case (5 participants)

| Duration | Caravan total | Per participant |
|---|---:|---:|
| Short | 41 | 8.2 |
| Medium | 108 | 21.6 |
| Long | 228 | 45.6 |

Therefore:

```text
Short:
Solo / best group per-participant = 10 / 10.5 = 95.2%
=> PASS against >= 90%

Medium:
Solo 40 > best group 33.5
=> PASS

Long:
Solo 120 > best group 86
=> PASS
```

### Net reward per participant per hour

Using 0.5h / 2h / 8h:

| Formation case | Short | Medium | Long |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Solo | 20.0 | 20.0 | 15.0 |
| Party, 2 participants | 21.0 | 16.75 | 10.75 |
| Caravan, 5 participants | 16.4 | 10.8 | 5.7 |

This also passes the intended shape:

- Short: best Party is only 5% above Solo,
- Medium: Solo is best individually,
- Long: Solo is clearly best individually.

### Relative gross-output advantage

Party / Solo:

```text
Short  2.20x
Medium 1.75x
Long   1.50x
```

Caravan / Solo:

```text
Short  4.50x
Medium 3.00x
Long   2.17x
```

Both decrease monotonically with duration and therefore pass criterion E.

## Acceptance outcome for current initial values

The ADR-008 / ADR-009 initial values pass this ADR-010 acceptance baseline.

This means the current simple model is internally consistent with the intended L0 shape; it does **not** mean the numbers are final or proven fun in gameplay.

## Simulator output contract

A future balance simulator should emit at least one row per scenario with fields equivalent to:

```text
formationClass
participantCount
durationClass
durationHours
grossReward
baseOperationalCost
costReduction
effectiveOperationalCost
netReward
netRewardPerParticipant
netRewardPerHour
netRewardPerParticipantPerHour
operationalCostShare
relativeGrossAdvantageVsSolo
relativePerParticipantEfficiencyVsSolo
acceptanceFlags[]
```

The implementation may use different naming, but the information content should remain available.

## Parameterization requirement

All acceptance calculations must consume model parameters rather than duplicate hard-coded balance values.

Conceptually:

```text
Scenario
  + CostParameters
  + RewardParameters
  + DurationParameters
  + FormationParameters
        ↓
  simulateScenario(...)
        ↓
  Metrics
        ↓
  evaluateAcceptance(...)
```

This preserves the path from today's fixed tables to future formula-based balancing.

## Deferred decisions

This ADR does not decide:

- final production durations,
- final production reward values,
- final formation size boundaries,
- Party/Caravan reward distribution rules,
- zone-specific tuning,
- rarity/drop Gold-equivalent valuation,
- risk premium,
- stamina interaction,
- Social Stress,
- progression/equipment economic effects,
- cost-reduction content,
- M6 scope.

## Result

L3 now has a complete initial evaluation loop:

```text
Cost Parameters + Reward Parameters
              ↓
         Scenario Simulation
              ↓
           Metrics
              ↓
      Acceptance Evaluation
              ↓
PASS / TUNE / FAIL
```

The next design action is to decide whether to implement this model in the existing balance simulator now, or continue defining other unresolved L0/L2/L3 game systems before selecting M6.
